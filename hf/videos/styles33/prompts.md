# styles33 prompts — DM for comment FABLE
Made with Claude Fable 5.2. Save as Claude Code commands (.claude/commands/<name>.md, "$ARGUMENTS" = your topic) or paste directly.
All three: 9:16, HTML canvas + GSAP, rendered frame by frame, ~6 s, your own drawings only (no logos, mascots or characters of any channel).

/paper-collage — Explain $ARGUMENTS as a full-screen paper photo-collage: layered depth (sky, far and near background, a blurred
foreground), one big cut-out subject with a white sticker edge, drop shadow and print halftone, a huge serif headline that sits
BEHIND the cut-out, one orange label, hand-drawn marker lines, then 3 torn cream paper tags (taped on, yellow highlighter on the
key word). Animate the ONE thing the fact is about. Paper grain over everything, slow parallax, stop-motion wobble at 12 fps.
  Example: /paper-collage why Pisa's tower doesn't fall  (the tower cut-out leans 5.5° → soil is drilled out from under the north side → it settles back to about 4°, "stable for 200+ years")

/flat-science — Explain $ARGUMENTS as a flat vector animation on a deep purple background: simple rounded shapes in 3 flat tones
each, soft glow on everything active, a faint network in the background for depth, floating particles, a slow camera push-in, pill
labels with a thin leader line, a bold white headline with one coloured word. Show cause → effect with colour and light.
  Example: /flat-science why tattoos last forever  (a needle drops ink under the skin → immune cells swallow it → one cell dies and drops the ink → a new cell swallows it again)

/painted-story — Tell $ARGUMENTS as a hand-painted storybook scene: limited palette, visible brush strokes and canvas grain, light
"boil" (texture changes at 8 fps), short captions on torn parchment strips, start close on the action and end with a slow
pull-back reveal.
  Example: /painted-story how the Hindenburg burned  (evening airship gliding to the mast → a spark at the tail → fire runs along the hull, the tail drops → the ground crew runs)

Note: a few rounds of feedback are normal; tell Claude what to change and it redraws.
