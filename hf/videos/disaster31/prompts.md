# disaster31 prompts — DM for comment DISASTER
Made with Claude Opus 5.5. Paste one into Claude Code (or save it as a command in .claude/commands/<name>.md). Each asks for a single
HTML file (three.js + GLSL shaders, everything drawn in code, no external images) that you can preview in the browser and render to a
9:16 mp4. It usually takes a few rounds of feedback (camera, colours, label positions) to get to the look in the video: that's normal.

## /explain-3d how a supervolcano erupts
Make a 5-second 9:16 (1080x1920, 30 fps) three.js animation of a supervolcano as a geology block diagram, cut open at the front.
- The cut face shows rock layers and a huge glowing magma chamber (animated noise, orange to yellow) with a feeder below it.
- 0–1.5 s: pressure builds, the chamber swells and the ground on top bulges up. 1.2–2 s: ring fractures glow and magma rises up them.
- 2.2–3.4 s: the roof block drops into the emptying chamber and leaves a caldera; grey ash columns rise from the ring.
- Slow camera push-in, then a pull-back that keeps the ash column in frame. Headline "Over 1,000 km³ of rock in one eruption."
  (USGS: a supereruption, VEI 8, releases more than 1,000 km³ at once). Labels: "Magma chamber", "Ring fractures", "Caldera – the ground collapses".

## /x-ray a tornado
Make a 5-second 9:16 three.js animation of a tornado in an x-ray / hologram look on a dark background with a faint blue grid floor.
- A translucent funnel (fresnel edges, spiral streaks rotating upward), a thin core line, a wide rotating cloud base above it with two
  orange rotation arrows (the rotating updraft), warm inflow spiralling in along the ground (moving dashes), and a debris cloud at the base.
- Slow orbit with a slight pull-back. Headline "Winds can top 300 mph." (radar measured about 318 mph in the 1999 Bridge Creek–Moore
  tornado, NWS Norman). Labels: "Rotating updraft", "Inflow – warm, moist air spirals in", "Debris cloud".

## /time-lapse a sinkhole opening
Make a 5-second 9:16 three.js animation: a road on top of a ground block cut open at the front (asphalt, clay/sand cover, limestone with
bedding and joints, blue groundwater flowing in the limestone).
- 0–2.5 s (a badge says "Years, sped up"): groundwater dissolves the limestone and a hidden cavity grows under the clay cover.
- 2.7–3.6 s (badge "Then: sudden collapse"): the cover drops into the cavity, the road breaks open into a pit, a bit of dust rises.
- Slow camera push-in. Headline "It can open in just hours." (USGS: cover-collapse sinkholes may develop abruptly, over a period of
  hours). Labels: "Groundwater – slowly dissolves limestone", "Hidden cavity – grows under the clay", "The roof gives way".
