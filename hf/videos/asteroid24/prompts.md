# asteroid24 prompts — DM for comment ASTEROID
Paste one into Claude Code (any recent Claude model). Each asks for a single HTML file (three.js or canvas, no external images except
public-domain NASA Blue Marble textures if you want them) that you can preview in the browser and render to a 9:16 mp4. It usually takes
a few rounds of feedback (camera, brightness, labels) to get to the look in the video: that's normal.

## /physics-sim an asteroid hitting the ocean
Make a 7-second 9:16 (1080x1920, 30 fps) three.js animation: an asteroid hits the Pacific, seen from low orbit.
- Realistic Earth (day texture, thin clouds, atmosphere rim), one warm sun light, starfield.
- A glowing asteroid streaks in at a shallow angle with a hot trail; on impact a flash, a white steam plume and ring waves that
  spread in every direction (time-lapsed, not to scale). Slow continuous camera push-in, tiny decaying shake.
- Headline "20 km every second." (typical impact speed ~17–20 km/s, NASA CNEOS). Labels with a dot: "Asteroid – about 20 km/s",
  "Ring wave – spreads in every direction", "Steam plume – water flashes to vapor".

## /what-if Earth lost its atmosphere
Make a 7-second 9:16 canvas animation of a lake landscape at noon losing its air.
- Start: blue sky, sun, mountains, lake with sun glitter. The sky drains to black and stars appear while the sun stays up (no air to
  scatter sunlight); the lake starts to boil and steam away (no pressure to hold liquid water). Slow drift, nothing static.
- Headline "The sky turns black." Labels: "Black sky – no air to scatter sunlight", "Oceans boil away – no pressure to hold them".

## /scale the tallest volcano on Mars versus Everest
Make a 6-second 9:16 canvas animation comparing heights to scale: Everest (8,849 m) grows first, then Olympus Mons (~21.9 km above
the Mars datum) rises behind it with a counting height label; a jet crosses at ~11 km for reference. Widths compressed.
- Headline "2.5× taller than Everest." Dashed km gridlines, labels "Everest – 8,849 m", "Olympus Mons – ~21.9 km, on Mars",
  "Jets cruise here – ~11 km". Dusty orange Mars sky, slow push-in.
