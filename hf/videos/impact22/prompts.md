Paste one into Claude Code (any recent Claude model). Each asks for a single HTML file (three.js or canvas, no external images)
that you can preview in the browser and render to a 9:16 mp4. It usually takes a few rounds of feedback (camera, brightness, labels)

## /physics-sim into a black hole
Make a 6-second 9:16 (1080x1920, 30 fps) three.js animation: an astronaut falls into a stellar-mass black hole.
- Black sphere for the shadow, a thin bright photon ring, an accretion disk (ring shader: white-hot inside → orange → dark red
  outside, swirling streaks, the side moving toward the camera brighter = Doppler beaming) and a camera-facing copy of the disk
  behind the hole as a hint of gravitational lensing. Starfield, subtle bloom, nothing blown out.
- Camera slightly above the disk plane, slow continuous push-in. The astronaut curves in from the top and is stretched along the
  line to the hole as it gets close (spaghettification), with a faint glowing streak, then fades at the edge.
- Labels with a dot: "Event horizon – not even light gets out", "Spaghettification – gravity stretches you".

## /what-if the Moon hit Earth
Make a 6-second 9:16 three.js animation of the Moon falling into Earth, both to true scale (Moon 3,474 km, Earth 12,742 km wide).
- Earth with land, ocean, clouds, night lights and a thin blue atmosphere rim; a cratered grey Moon; one warm sun light.
- The Moon accelerates in, squashes into the crust; a glowing molten patch spreads from the impact with a bright shockwave ring;
  thousands of glowing debris particles fly out and arc back. Slow camera push-in, a tiny decaying shake at impact.
- Labels: "Moon – 3,474 km wide", "Earth – 12,742 km wide", "Impact – debris thrown into space".

## /deep-dive inside a volcano
Make a 6-second 9:16 canvas animation: a cutaway of an erupting stratovolcano, then the camera dives down to the magma chamber.
- Cone made of alternating layers of ash and old lava, rock strata underground, a glowing conduit, a magma chamber a few km down
  with rising bubbles; lava flows down both flanks, lava-fountain sparks and a rising, spreading ash plume at the crater.
- One continuous eased camera move: start on the crater, pull back while diving down the conduit, push in on the chamber.
- Labels: "Ash plume", "Conduit – magma's way up", "Magma chamber – 700–1,200 °C".
